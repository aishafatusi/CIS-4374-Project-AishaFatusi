Areas Where AI Was Used

1. Competitor/Market Brainstorming
AI was used to brainstorm examples of existing parking technology platforms and organize them by the main problem they solve.
Prompt:
What parking technology platforms currently exist? Give me examples of apps that help drivers find or reserve parking, real-time parking occupancy/sensor systems, and parking garage management software. Briefly explain what each platform does.

This as well as some online searches helped identify examples such as SpotHero, ParkWhiz, ParkMobile, Google Maps/Waze, Cleverciti, Nwave, Metropolis, SKIDATA, TIBA, and Flowbird.

What I kept:
The general competitor categories.
Relevant examples of existing parking platforms.
The idea that most platforms focus primarily on one part of the parking experience.

What I changed:
The examples were organized into the three categories used in the final document.
Descriptions were shortened and rewritten to fit the project's specific comparison.
The final conclusions about LenuxParks' competitive opportunity were determined by me.


2. Requirements Brainstorming
AI was used as a brainstorming tool to identify possible functional and non-functional requirements for a parking platform.
Prompt:
What functional requirements would a mobile parking application and an administrative parking management dashboard need? Include features for drivers, parking operators, administrators, payments, availability, and reporting.
This helped brainstorm requirements such as registration, real-time availability, interactive maps, reservations, digital payments, receipts, notifications, administration, analytics, and payment reconciliation.

What I kept:
Requirements that fit the project's stated vision and scope.
Features that were relevant to either drivers or parking operators.

What I changed:
Requirements were selected and reorganized based on the actual LenuxParks project.
Specific requirements and performance targets were reviewed by me, also taking into account information from lecture 2 about SRS and use cases


3. Use Case Brainstorming

AI was used to help brainstorm possible interactions between different users and the system.
Prompt:
Create a list of possible use cases for a parking platform that has a driver mobile app and an administrative dashboard for parking operators. Include actors, basic steps, preconditions, postconditions, user stories, and acceptance criteria.
This helped identify possible use cases involving drivers, parking operators, system administrators, finance personnel, payment providers, and city transportation departments.

What I kept:
The general structure of the use cases.
Relevant actors and interactions.
The use of user stories and acceptance criteria to describe requirements.

What I changed:
I selected the use cases that were relevant to LenuxParks.
Steps, user stories, and acceptance criteria were rewritten and adjusted to fit the project's specific functionality.
The final 15 use cases were organized according to given project requirements and how professor did it in SRS lecture slides.
